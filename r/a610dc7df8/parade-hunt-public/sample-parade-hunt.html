1 Brand-New / Newborn Survival Dads

CONFIDENCE PICK
Dads 0–12 months into their first baby. Wrecked, wired on coffee, secretly proud, drowning in firsts. They live in the gap between 'I would die for this kid' and 'I have not slept since March.' The crowd never shrinks — last year's survivors graduate out and a fresh wave marches in behind them.

Why it keeps refilling (the replenishment engine)

Every stage is a new inside joke: the newborn fog, the first blowout, the 4-month sleep regression, the first sick day, the first word. A dad doesn't buy one shirt and quit — each milestone earns a new stanza. And it's heavily gift-driven (Father's Day, baby showers, 'first Father's Day'), so the buyer often isn't even the dad.

Who's already in the lane

  • Amazon / Etsy: flooded with generic 'Sleep Deprived New Dad' text tees — high volume, zero brand, zero posture.
  • No Shopify identity-brand has flag-planted here. The lane is wide open for a real voice.

Design wedges

  • Self-deprecating survival humor — lean into the exhaustion, not the Hallmark sentiment.
  • The 'firsts' as a serialized in-joke (one shirt per milestone).
  • Coffee + no-sleep iconography fused with new-dad identity.
  • Quiet pride underneath the chaos — the 'I'm terrible at this and I love it' tone.

2 Beginner Woodworkers

CONFIDENCE PICK
People in their first 1–2 years of woodworking. Garage hobbyists, weekend warriors, the 'I bought a circular saw and a dream' crowd. They are gloriously bad at it and they know it — and that self-awareness is the whole personality.

Why it keeps refilling (the replenishment engine)

Woodworking is a wardrobe-ceiling risk for *experts* — but beginners are a different animal. Every botched cut, glued finger, and crooked first project is a fresh joke, and the skill ladder means there's always a next embarrassment. New people pour in constantly (it's one of the most-searched beginner hobbies). The identity is 'lovable disaster,' which never gets old.

Who's already in the lane

  • Amazon/Etsy/TeePublic/Zazzle: 'measure twice cut once' dad-merch everywhere — generic, no posture.
  • Chummy Tees and similar do pun shirts but no flag-planted *beginner* identity brand.
  • Wide open for a self-mocking 'we're all bad at this together' voice.

Design wedges

  • The lovable-disaster posture — celebrate the failures, not the masterpieces.
  • Beginner-specific in-jokes (the expert merch ignores this crowd entirely).
  • 'Measure twice, cut three times' energy as a brand voice, not a one-off pun.
  • Sawdust-as-identity — the mess is the badge.

3 The Whittling / Hand-Carving Crowd

REPLENISHMENT PICK
People who carve by hand — spoons, comfort birds, little figures — usually for the meditative quiet of it. A lot of overlap with bushcraft, GCSE/teen carvers, and 'I needed something to do with my hands' adults. Slower, gentler energy than power-tool woodworking.

Why it keeps refilling (the replenishment engine)

Two engines: (1) the gear — knives, strops, basswood, gloves — is endlessly collectible and flex-worthy, and (2) every finished carving is a tiny new identity stamp. It's a 'practice forever, never finished' craft, so the in-jokes compound. The calm/meditative angle also pulls in a stress-relief crowd the loud-tool merch ignores.

Who's already in the lane

  • Schaaf Tools sells some carver merch; Amazon/TeePublic have generic 'whittle by whittle' puns.
  • No identity brand owns the calm/meditative-carver posture. Very open.

Design wedges

  • Calm-craft / 'occupied hands, quiet mind' posture — the anti-hustle hobby.
  • Gear-flex for knives, strops, basswood (the collectible angle).
  • Beginner-friendly 'practice on a bar of soap' in-jokes.
  • Tiny-wins identity — 'I made a slightly-lumpy spoon and I'm proud.'

4 The 'Just One More Tool' Crowd

REPLENISHMENT PICK
Tool-obsessed makers and dads whose hobby is half woodworking, half collecting the means to woodwork. The 'I needed a reason to buy the tool, so I started the project' crowd. Brand loyalty runs deep (the battery-platform tribes are real).

Why it keeps refilling (the replenishment engine)

It's the purest replenishment engine on the board: there is *always* one more tool, so the in-joke literally never closes. 'Too many tools' is a running gag with its own memes. The identity is acquisitive and self-aware, and it's wildly gift-driven (spouses buying 'the tool he won't buy himself'). Repeat-purchase behavior is baked into the personality.

Who's already in the lane

  • Tons of generic 'you can never have too many tools' POD; brand-platform fan merch exists but no neutral identity brand.
  • Open for a posture-led 'tool addict, and proud' voice that isn't tied to one manufacturer.

Design wedges

  • 'Just one more tool' as the entire brand spine (endless serialized gag).
  • The justification-comedy — 'I bought the tool first, found the project later.'
  • Spouse-gift angle ('the tool he won't buy himself').
  • Battery-platform tribal humor (without naming a brand, to stay clean).

5 The Dad-Who-Escapes-to-the-Garage

DARK HORSE
The tired dad whose sanctuary is the garage — the man cave, the workshop, the place he disappears to the moment the baby goes down. Half about making things, half about the 20 minutes of quiet. This is the literal intersection of 'sleep-deprived new dad' and 'nap-time woodworker.'

Why it keeps refilling (the replenishment engine)

The identity is emotional, not just functional — 'the garage is the one place that's mine.' That's a feeling people re-buy. As the kids grow, the garage story evolves (escape → teaching the kid → the kid taking over the bench). It's also a strong gift lane: families know exactly who 'garage dad' is. The catch — and the opportunity — is that the self-mocking voice barely exists online, so whoever plants the flag *defines* it.

Who's already in the lane

  • Generic 'dad's garage / man cave' signs and merch exist, but no posture-led identity brand.
  • Because the wedge isn't pre-written, there's almost no direct competition for a real voice here.

Design wedges

  • The garage-as-sanctuary emotional angle (re-buyable feeling, not a one-off joke).
  • The fusion: exhausted dad + maker — nobody is serving this exact overlap.
  • The evolving story (escape now → teaching the kid later).
  • Quiet-pride tone over loud-tool bravado — differentiates from generic man-cave merch.

Pick the one you’re drawn to, then the next step is to narrow it to a single pocket inside the crowd — the exact sub-group big enough to be worth it and easy to sell to — so your designs land dead-on instead of spread thin.
